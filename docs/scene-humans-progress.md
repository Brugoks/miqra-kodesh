# Human implementation status

The earlier completion claims in this file were stronger than the shipped assets justified. The previous Capernaum actor GLBs were static primitive assemblies, while other scenes did not load a human asset group. The earlier detail-level field did not select geometry and the actor budget could hide a person without restoring its fallback.

## Implemented

- Three textured MakeHuman-derived GLBs with anatomical bodies and faces, eyes, hair, fitted tunics and real skeletal animation.
- Shared asset loading across all four scenes and replacement at the actual existing crowd positions.
- Real near/medium mesh selection, bounded nearby animation, independent clone rigs, distance-synchronized gait, and fallback restoration.
- Reduced-motion route/animation freezing, height-aware character clearance queries and navigation integration.
- Source licenses, archive hashes, pinned authoring tools, reproducible Blender build and binary/image validation.
- **Procedural gait and pose** (`sceneHumanClips.js`), replacing the baked clips as the thing actually driving a character's skeleton. The baked `walk` clip's ankle travelled 0.31m against a 1.15m stride (half of every metre walked was the feet sliding), and every baked clip put the forearm 50-70 degrees off vertical because the authoring script (`scripts/humans/build_characters.py`) posed each bone in world axes with no regard for the elbow. The procedural clips are authored as an absolute angle from vertical per limb, converted through real forward kinematics — deliberately not a local delta on top of the bind pose, because the three characters do not share one: the artisan's hips sit 9 degrees off true, the villager's spine 21 degrees straighter. The baked clips remain the fallback for a rig that doesn't have the bones this needs (a test mock, say); see `docs/scene-humans-motion-and-crowding-plan.md` for the full measurement-by-measurement account.
- **A real route model** (`sceneRoutes.js`), shared by the fallback crowd and the GLB actors. `figure.speed` used to be route-fractions per second, which meant an 86m Capernaum lane was walked at a 3-5.6 m/s sprint and a 7m one at a 0.25 m/s shuffle; it is metres per second now, with a real accelerate/cruise/decelerate profile and a stop-turn-continue at each end instead of an instant direction reversal.
- **Crowd placement that respects the navigation mesh.** Capernaum's villagers now stand on the real `floorAt`, not a flat two-level guess (four of the thirteen shore villagers used to float up to 0.52m above the ramp); they keep at least 0.62m of personal space (the shore haunt's closest pair used to be 0.08m); they stand in knots of two to four facing what they're doing rather than one crowd facing its own centre (the shore haunt used to be thirteen people in one 3m ring); and every walking route is audited against `blockerAt` before being trusted (three of the five previous routes ran through solid buildings, one for over half its length).
- **A thin procedural overlay** (`sceneHumanPose.js`) on top of the mixer's output — breathing, an occasional weight shift, and a head that glances around and toward a nearby visitor — so a figure standing still for ten seconds reads as a loop less than a bare animation clip does. Seeded per actor so neighbours don't move in step; contributes nothing under reduced motion (the caller never invokes it).

See [asset documentation](scene-humans-assets.md) for the exact asset contract and rebuild commands, and `docs/scene-humans-motion-and-crowding-plan.md` for the motion and crowding work above.

## Not claimed complete

Scanned photoreal likenesses, skin subsurface scattering, authored period-specific wardrobe for every era, hand-to-prop constraints (`placement.props` is declared per character but never instantiated — `RIG_DEFINITIONS.sockets` is empty and nothing reads the field), foot IK, cloth simulation, and mobile GPU certification are not implemented. The shared models are an improvement in anatomical and material detail; “ultra realistic” is a visual target, not a status that unit tests can certify.

`HumanAnimationController.updateBlink()` is dead code against the shipped assets: all three GLBs have zero morph targets (`build_characters.py` calls `shape_key_remove(all=True, apply_mix=True)` during the bake), so the blink tests in `sceneHumanAnimation.test.js` pass only against a synthetic mesh built for the test and certify nothing about what actually ships.

The procedural gait is tuned against two of the three shipped skeletons (artisan, villager — the two with meaningfully different bind poses) and against real bone-length data, not eyeballed; it has not been checked visually, per this project's working preference of not spending a session on visual verification. Ground clearance during the swing phase is real (previously near-zero) but on the generous side of realistic (~18-30cm depending on the character's leg length, against a real gait's 10-15cm) — a plausible thing to tighten by eye, not a defect a test can flag on its own. Kneeling is an approximate pose, not verified against any reference; a kneeling figure's legs are also mostly hidden under the robe geometry, which is the main reason this wasn't pursued further. `carrying` has no dedicated walk variant — a figure carrying something while it walks plays the same shared `walk` clip as everyone else, since `HumanAnimationController`'s state machine picks a walk clip by movement alone, not by activity; only the standing `carry` pose is distinct. `sceneRoutes.js`'s lane offset is fixed in world space rather than flipped by direction of travel (an earlier version that did flip it produced a real teleport-sized position jump at every dwell-to-leg boundary — see the test named for exactly that in `sceneRoutes.test.js`), so two walkers sharing one route in opposite directions do not reliably keep to separate sides of it.
